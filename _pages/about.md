I am currently a Postdoctoral Research Fellow in the Department of Epidemiology at Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health (HSPH). My research focuses on aging and cardiovascular epidemiology, aiming to advance the prevention of cardiovascular and brain diseases such as stroke and dementia. I have extensive academic training and research experience in preventive medicine, epidemiology and population health. During my past two years of postdoctoral training under the mentorship of Dr. Yuan Ma, I have led several projects clarifying vascular contributions to cognitive impairment and dementia in older adults. My work has resulted in four publications in prestigious clinical journals (two in Hypertension, one in Alzheimer’s & Dementia, and one in The Journals of Gerontology: Series A), and a few other projects in the pipeline. In one of the projects, we found that targeting hypotensive episodes, identified through 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM), may offer promising strategies for the early prevention of cognitive impairment. The paper has been published in Hypertension with an accompanying editorial titled “How Low Can You Go? Flagging a Role for Hypotension in Cognitive Decline with Intensive Blood Pressure Therapy” that further emphasizes the novelty and important clinical implications of monitoring and managing hypotensive episodes, in addition to hypertension, in older adults. These experiences have inspired my long-term goal—to establish an independent research program in cardiovascular and aging epidemiology, integrating 24-hour ABPM, neuroimaging, neuropathology, and cognitive outcomes to inform precision prevention of dementia and aging-related disorders.

With my expertise intersecting preventive medicine and aging epidemiology and successful experience in leading 24-hour ABPM and cognition projects, I believe I am in an excellent position to accomplish the proposed postdoctoral fellowship.
